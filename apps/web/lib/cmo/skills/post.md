# Writing a post

One skill for every platform. What makes a post good is the same everywhere: one true thing, said the way the founder would say it. What differs (length, what shows before "more", line breaks, hashtags, links) is in the <platform> block; follow it.

## What a post is
- **One true thing the founder did, saw, decided or learned**, with the detail that makes it theirs: a number, a name, a date, a before and after, a mistake. The detail comes from the documents, the brief or the founder's notes, never from you.
- **The calendar item is a topic, not a headline.** Never reuse its wording as the first line. "Why manual approval is the secret to AI marketing" is a topic; the post says what the founder actually does and what happened.
- **Help or tell, don't pitch.** The product appears only when it is the plain answer to the post's problem. Most posts do not name it. A post that only describes the product is an ad; write it only when the calendar item asks for a launch or an update.

## The first line
- It says the most interesting true thing straight away, in plain words. No formula, no tease, no promise of a reveal.
- Good first lines are specific: "We stopped sending drafts to a scheduler and approve each one by hand." Bad ones are formulas: "Here's why…", "The secret to…", "The hidden cost of…", "Most founders get this wrong:".
- On a platform that cuts at "more", the first line lands before the cut.

## The rest
- Say it the way the founder would say it to one person: short or long as the point needs, their words, their rhythm. Vary the shape between the three versions; three posts of the same shape read as a machine.
- End on the last concrete point, a takeaway the reader can use, or what the founder will do next. Do not end with a question to bait replies, a recap, or a one-line moral.
- No links and no hashtags in a post. No emoji unless the founder uses them. No exclamation marks unless the founder uses them.

## The founder's voice
- Their rules (<founder_rules>) and the way they edit (<founder_voice>) come first. When their edits show a habit (a phrase, a rhythm, a dash), keep it: it is their voice.
- With no voice notes yet, write plainly: a sharp person talking, not a brand. Their later edits teach the rest.

## Three versions
- Three different takes on the same true thing (a story, a lesson, a plain statement of what changed), not three hooks on the same text.

## Before you hand it over
- Every number and claim is in the documents, the brief or the founder's notes.
- It passes the no-ai-slop guidance. Our code refuses its stock patterns (contrasts like "not X, it's Y", colon reveals, headline formulas, hype words, dashes between clauses, a closing question), so a version that uses one comes back to you.
- It is not a near-copy of a recent post.
