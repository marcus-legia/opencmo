Schedule and Publish

Status: built (main at 4f70a59, 2026-11-08). Designed 2026-10-26, built in phases P0–P3 (P4–P6) and the CMO review (#169–#180). Markdown twin for agents: schedule-publish.md; the two say the same thing. Parent pages: ai-cmo.html, approval.html.

Two departments that run back to back, the last step of the owner's sketch (2026-11-08). Schedule owns time: when each approved deliverable goes out, on which platform and account. Publish owns the door to the platforms: one adapter that posts through Zernio at the approved time, or hands the founder a ready composer when no account is connected.

§1

Verdict

  • Built: every platform through Zernio, X included (owner, 2026-10-26). The founder connects each account once through Zernio's OAuth link; at the approved time the worker posts through Zernio and a signed webhook confirms. Tokens stay with Zernio; we never build a platform app.
  • Still "assist, never act": nothing goes out without the founder's approval of content, time and account, on the founder's own account. The copy says "you approve when it posts".
  • Live only with the owner's switches: ZERNIO_API_KEY with OPENCMO_PUBLISH_LIVE=1, and ZERNIO_WEBHOOK_SECRET (the Costs page shows both). Still to check before it is sold: Zernio's TikTok audit (or TikTok posts go private), its real price per connected account, and its terms for a SaaS reselling posting.
  • The intent link is the fallback for a founder with no connected account, or when Zernio is down. X API cost passes through: ~$0.015 a post, ~$0.20 with a link.

§4

From approved to live

Scroll sideways to see the whole diagram.

  • Schedule: deliverable (approved) → proposed slot (best time per platform, founder's timezone, gaps, caps) → schedule card (platform, account, time; all three approved) → waiting (an edit raises a new card) → due (Modal sweep, every minute, lease).
  • Publish mode (an account is connected): due → publishing → the worker uploads the media to Zernio (publish_media), then posts → signed webhook (status, post id, failure reason) → published → metrics after 24 h.
  • Intent mode (no connected account for that platform): due → a notice (push, email, in-app: "time to post on X") → the founder posts through the intent link with the text filled in → published (paste the link, or metrics find it). TikTok, YouTube and Instagram have no web composer that takes a video: their card asks the founder to connect the account.
  • One adapter: publish(deliverable, slot) → { status, external_id, url, error }; the brain and event rules never know which path ran.

§5

Schedule

As built (schedule_slots):

id, user_id
deliverable_id   the approved post, short, slideshow…
platform         x | threads | linkedin | tiktok | instagram | youtube
account_id       → social_accounts; null in intent mode
mode             publish | intent
publish_at       UTC; shown in the founder's timezone
window_ends_at   past it the slot is missed, never posted late
status           proposed | waiting | due | reminded | publishing | published | missed | failed | cancelled
approval_id      the schedule card
jitter_seconds   ±240, natural posting
options          platform fields (TikTok privacy and toggles, the AI-generated label)
idempotency_key, lease_until, attempts, media_task_id
external_id, external_url, error, published_at

How a time is picked (schedule_rules(), best_time_rules()):

  • Timezone first: profiles.timezone, set at onboarding from the browser, editable.
  • Default slots per platform from Buffer's 2026 study, stored as data (schedule-defaults.json).
  • The founder's own data takes over once a platform has 10 published posts with metrics (best median views per hour, last 90 days).
  • Gaps and caps: 3 hours between two posts on one platform; daily caps X 5, Threads 2, LinkedIn 1, TikTok 2, Instagram 1, YouTube 2; up to 3 proposals over the next 14 days.
  • A short and its long video are not posted on the same platform the same day.
  • Rules are code; no model picks the time.

The clock is Modal's sweep: it claims due slots with a lease and starts the publish job or the reminder. A slot past its window becomes missed and raises a new card; it never posts late. Moving an item changes the time and supersedes its card.

Measures

Slots posted on time (remind mode: within an hour of the notice) · missed and failed slots with reasons · median views per slot hour per platform (feeds the best-time pick) · posts per week per platform against cmo_goals.

§10

Build phases

All built: P0 account connect, timezone, a Zernio profile per founder · P1 scheduled posting through Zernio, schedule cards, signed webhooks, the intent fallback · P2 best time, jitter, gaps and caps · P3 metrics through Zernio.

What is left, ranked by the CMO loop (owner, 2026-11-08): the owner's Zernio checks (TikTok audit, price, terms) and live keys; then a founder approves one post and schedules it within the first minutes (plan §4, "done when").
