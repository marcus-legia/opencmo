Approval: the founder's department

Status: built (main at 4f70a59, 2026-11-08). Designed 2026-10-26, built in phases A0–A3 (P1–P6) and the CMO review (#169–#180). Markdown twin for agents: approval.md; the two say the same thing. Parent pages: ai-cmo.html, brain.html, studio.html.

Approval is where "assist, never act" is enforced. Every department raises a card; nothing goes out, and nothing paid runs, until the founder approves exactly what the card shows. In the owner's sketch of 2026-11-08 there are two approvals: the words (plan, posts, slide text, briefs) and then what was made from them (finished video, shorts, slideshow), each followed by the schedule.

§4

Design: one table, one decide RPC, three surfaces

Scroll sideways to see the whole diagram.

Cards appear where the founder already is (inbox for the brain's work, chat for work started in the editor, confirm code for MCP); the brain's cards are rows of one table decided by one RPC.

  • Raised by: Brain (week plan, goal, corrections), Writer (posts, captions), Sales (reply drafts), Studio (prices, finished video, shorts batch, slides), Schedule (time + account per slot).
  • Seen in: Approval inbox (web and phone, grouped by department); the CMO chat (the same card); Assistant chat card (when started in the editor); MCP confirm (signed code, 600 s); reminders (digest, email, web push; open the card, never decide).
  • Decided by: approval_decide (wraps cmo_decide_post, cmo_decide_opportunity, cmo_decide_goal, cmo_decide_correction): owner only under RLS, advisory lock, idempotent, checks the snapshot is current, daily outbound caps.
  • Outcomes: approved → event rule → next order; edited then approved → similarity + edit mode → cmo_draft_outcomes; sent back → reason → the order replans once (repeated → correction); skipped → memory; expired → a new card.

The table as built:

id, user_id
kind            post | reply | goal | script | price | finished_video | shorts_batch
                | slideshow | schedule | correction | plan
department      brain | research | writer | studio | sales | schedule | publish
ref_kind, ref_id  content_item | opportunity | goal | correction | studio_step
                | editor_project | short_batch | schedule_slot | work_order

price_credits, post_at, account
status          waiting | held | approved | edited | skipped | expired | cancelled
decided_via     app | email | push | mcp
reason, expires_at, held_until, released_at, created_at, decided_at

As built, a superseded card is cancelled, a sent-back card is recorded with its reason on the row it refers to, and batching is by row (one short_batch for the shorts of one video) rather than a batch_id.

§6

Rules

  1. You approve exactly what you saw. The snapshot holds the content hash (JCS), price, time and account; any change after the card was raised supersedes it and raises a new one.
  2. Outbound and paid never auto-approve. Only free, internal, reversible items may skip a card, and only on opt-in.
  3. Few cards. Batch what belongs together (shorts of one video, slides of one slideshow, posts of one day); keep the daily count low.
  4. Cards expire. Schedule cards when their time passes; price cards when the price changes or after a set time (MCP: a confirm code lasts 600 s). Expired cards ask again; they never run late.
  5. Reminders never decide. Email or push opens the card; no approve button in an email (mail scanners open links).
  6. Every decision teaches. Approve-as-is, edits and their mode, send-back and skip reasons go to the brain's memory.

§8

Learning from decisions

Every card kind records its outcome: X posts and the other platforms record the version chosen, approved-text similarity and the edit mode (cmo_draft_outcomes, classify-edits.ts); skip reasons become memories; a Studio send-back becomes a lesson (approvals_studio_lesson). Repeated edit modes are counted by a trigger (cmo_draft_outcomes_learn) and return as a correction card (brain tier 0 memory).
